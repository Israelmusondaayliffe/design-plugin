# Atlassian Design System

## Visual thesis

Atlassian's transferable strength is product-family coherence. Foundations, tokens, and components create a grammar that can serve different collaboration products without every team inventing spacing, states, or interaction patterns again.

## Signature relationships

- **8-pixel rhythm.** Spacing is systematic enough to make dense product layouts feel related.
- **Foundations feed components.** Color, type, grid, elevation, and iconography are not independent styling layers.
- **Semantic tokens protect meaning.** Product changes should flow through roles rather than scattered literal values.
- **Cross-product recognition.** Similar jobs should look and behave similarly across the product family.
- **State clarity.** Interactive states matter as much as static component appearance.

## Layout and density

Use a repeatable spacing rhythm and grid to support medium-to-high product density. Reserve large spacing jumps for real hierarchy. Avoid a card for every grouping when alignment and section structure already communicate relationships.

## Typography

Typography should support rapid scanning in collaborative workflows. Separate page hierarchy, control labels, metadata, and long-form content. Do not use display treatments to make routine operational surfaces feel more branded.

## Color and surfaces

Color should be tokenized by role and state. Brand color can be visible in navigation or primary emphasis, but status, selection, focus, and text roles must remain semantically stable.

## Components and interaction

Components should inherit shared tokens and preserve predictable behavior across dialogs, menus, tables, navigation, forms, and collaboration patterns. A local exception should be justified by a distinct job, not taste drift.

## Adaptation rules

- Preserve spatial rhythm, semantic tokens, state clarity, and cross-surface consistency.
- Adapt brand palette and typography only if the replacement maintains comparable role coverage.
- Use the shared grammar more strongly in product UI than in marketing surfaces.

## Failure modes

- Treating 8 pixels as a magic number while ignoring hierarchy.
- Mixing local colors and radii that bypass semantic tokens.
- Making each product area visually unique at the cost of recognizability.
- Copying Atlassian blue or components without the underlying interaction grammar.

## Evidence boundary

This case is original analysis of public Atlassian Design System guidance. No Atlassian screenshots or proprietary product assets are included.
