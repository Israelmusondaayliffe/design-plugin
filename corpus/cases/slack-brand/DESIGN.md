# Slack Brand

## Visual thesis

Human language and color make workplace software feel social without hiding its utility. A recognizable multi-color identity stays bounded while text and surfaces remain quieter.

## Collaboration warmth in practice

Friendly narrative blocks anchored by product conversation evidence. Human language and color make workplace software feel social without hiding its utility.

A recognizable multi-color identity stays bounded while text and surfaces remain quieter.

Product examples show exchange, coordination, and shared context instead of generic dashboards. Calls to action and explanations reduce formality without becoming vague.

Subtle message and state transitions.

## Adaptation rules

1. Suitable contexts: team products that need to communicate social coordination, and workplace marketing with credible product conversation examples.
2. Preserve: collaboration warmth and brand mark discipline.
3. Poor fit: formal public services where a conversational brand posture could weaken authority.

## Failure modes

1. Scattering all brand colors across controls and status states.
2. Using playful copy where permissions or data handling need precision.
3. Showing decorative chat bubbles without real workflow proof.

## Evidence confidence and gaps

Only source identity is classified as directly observed. The design relationships are source-bounded analyst interpretations, and the adaptation rules are recommendations. Internal metrics, unpublished decisions, and closed-source implementation details were not inspected.
