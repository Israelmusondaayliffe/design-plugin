1. **health-service clarity.** Questions and instructions use plain language under stress and uncertainty.
2. **one-decision pacing.** Pages limit competing actions so users can understand what is being asked.
3. **error recovery beside input.** Validation explains the problem and the route to correction at the relevant field.

## Adaptation rules

1. Suitable contexts: public health and high-stakes service flows, and forms used by broad audiences under variable cognitive load.
2. Preserve: health-service clarity and one-decision pacing.
3. Poor fit: expressive campaign pages where a strict service rhythm would suppress the intended voice.

## Failure modes

1. Combining several unrelated questions on one screen.
2. Using clinical jargon instead of user language.
3. Reporting an error without saying how to fix it.
