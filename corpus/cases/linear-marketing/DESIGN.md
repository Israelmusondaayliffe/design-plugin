# Linear Marketing Site

## Visual thesis

Start with controlled luminosity, then test whether quiet dark canvas still governs the result. Dark product narrative with cropped interface evidence and one restrained luminous focus.

## Signature relationships

- **Quiet dark canvas.** Low-chroma surfaces create room for interface evidence.
- **Product-led narrative.** Screens appear at the exact point where a claim needs proof.
- **Controlled luminosity.** Light and glow guide focus without becoming a general effect.
- **Compressed copy.** Short statements keep the pace closer to a product demo than a brochure.

## Editorial composition and proof

Staggered proof windows along a narrow story column. The page or screen must preserve product-led narrative as the container changes. A useful trial covers evaluation and conversion, plus browsing and discovery with no borrowed source assets.

## Type, color, and art direction

Use a neutral sans hierarchy to separate quiet dark canvas from controlled luminosity. Media should provide real product evidence beside its supporting claim in service of product-led narrative. Recheck every destination color against compressed copy.

## Narrative behavior and access

Keep quiet dark canvas intact through state changes. Short staged reveals tied to section entry.

Public material for Linear Marketing Site supports only a baseline access assessment; the destination must prove the rest. The minimum access contract for Linear Marketing Site is concrete: semantic order for quiet dark canvas, visible focus for product-led narrative, non-color meaning for controlled luminosity, and input parity that does not erase compressed copy. Also test reflow and reduced motion against this risk: using dark mode as a fashionable default without sufficient product evidence.

## Adaptation rules

Keep: Quiet dark canvas. Low-chroma surfaces create room for interface evidence.

Translate: Product-led narrative. Screens appear at the exact point where a claim needs proof.

Test in: workflow SaaS with a polished product worth showing and technical products that need a premium but not luxury-coded posture.

Avoid in: content-heavy services where readers need long explanations and daylight reading comfort.

## Failure modes

**Failure 1.** Using dark mode as a fashionable default without sufficient product evidence.

**Failure 2.** Covering screenshots with haze until the content becomes unreadable.

**Failure 3.** Animating every section instead of revealing only meaningful relationships.
