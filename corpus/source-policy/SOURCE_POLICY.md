# Corpus Source and Publication Policy

## Purpose

The corpus exists to teach and retrieve design knowledge through original analysis. It is not a screenshot archive, a copied design library, a mirror of another reference service, or a substitute for proprietary documentation.

## Allowed source classes

A case may be created from public websites inspected through ordinary access, public design systems and brand guidelines, open-source products, user-owned products, client-authorized products, user-provided references when use is permitted, and original Design plugin work.

## Disallowed acquisition and storage

- Do not bulk-copy Refero DESIGN.md records or other proprietary reference catalogs.
- Do not scrape a service in violation of its terms or access controls.
- Do not store secrets, credentials, personal data, client-confidential material, or private project content in the public corpus.
- Do not represent an inference as a measured fact.
- Do not publish third-party logos, photography, screenshots, illustrations, or proprietary copy merely because they are visible on a public website.

## Truth classes

Every evidence-backed claim uses one of these classes: `observed`, `measured`, `inferred`, `estimated`, `recommended`, or `unknown`.

## Publication states

- `private`: development or authorized internal work only.
- `review`: candidate for publication but not yet cleared.
- `public`: original analysis cleared for public distribution.
- `blocked`: must not be published until the identified issue is resolved.

## Visual evidence

Public cases default to an original abstract preview generated from `preview-spec.json`. Source screenshots may be linked or displayed only when rights and publication review permit it. The source URL remains the primary path for inspecting the original design.

## Review standard

Before a case becomes public, verify source and study date, originality, evidence coverage, explicit uncertainty, token-role integrity, asset rights, useful and unsuitable contexts, and enough design specificity to distinguish the case from generic style adjectives.
