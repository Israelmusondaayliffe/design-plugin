02 / Method

How to trust what you see

Evidence is a boundary, not a badge.

A useful reference should tell you more than "this looks good." It should show what the source says, what we think it means, what we suggest trying, and what the inspected public material does not establish.

Four evidence labels

Know what kind of claim is in front of you.

What the source says Observed
Directly supported by a cited public source.
What we think it means Inferred
Our reading of a source pattern, not the source owner's stated intent.
What we suggest trying Recommended
Adaptation guidance, not a promise that the choice will work.
What we cannot prove Unknown
Not established by the public material we inspected.

Originality boundary

Borrow relationships, not identity.

Study how parts work together: hierarchy, density, states, language, or navigation. Then make a new choice for your own problem. Do not copy source geometry, branded colors, fonts, logos, screenshots, or decorative assets.
